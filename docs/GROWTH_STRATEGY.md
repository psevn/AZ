# AZ — Growth & Student Acquisition Strategy

**Status:** Initial strategic framework — hypotheses to research and validate  
**Product:** AZ by EZORD  
**First launch market:** Qatar  
**Product ambition:** Build AZ as a globally capable academic platform from the beginning, launching first in Qatar and expanding across the Arab world and internationally as readiness and evidence allow  
**Last updated:** 2026-11-05

> This is a living strategy document, not an approved launch plan. Ideas below are hypotheses and options for research. They do not automatically enter the V1 product scope or authorize spending.

## Product and market principle

**Global by Design, Launching from Qatar.** Qatar is the first go-to-market beachhead, not the product's permanent geographic boundary or the template every other country must follow. Product foundations should support international learners without requiring a redesign for each new market. Market expansion remains staged: Qatar beta, Gulf markets, wider Arab markets, then selected global markets. These stages guide distribution and validation; they do not mean the product should be technically restricted to one region between launches.

## 1. Growth ambition

AZ should earn attention by being genuinely useful to students, easy to recommend, and distinctive enough to remember.

The growth system should connect five stages:

**Discover → Try → Experience value → Share → Return**

The aim is not attention for its own sake. Growth should bring in students who reach a meaningful learning outcome, continue using AZ, and recommend it because it helps them.

## 2. Growth principles

1. **Student value before virality.** The product experience must justify every referral.
2. **Trust before reach.** No misleading claims, fake scarcity, fabricated testimonials, or promises of grades/exam predictions.
3. **Reward quality, not raw sign-ups.** Incentives should be tied to verified, meaningful milestones.
4. **Respect students and institutions.** No spam, forced contact uploads, or unauthorized use of university names/logos.
5. **Measure incrementality and economics.** Track whether a campaign brings genuinely new, retained, paying students at a sustainable cost.
6. **Launch narrow; design globally.** Validate the first launch in Qatar, but do not hard-code Qatar's institutions, academic calendar, language, currency, or assumptions into shared product foundations. Prepare the product architecture for multiple countries from the start.
7. **Protect the MVP.** Growth experiments should not delay core learning workflows or become V1 requirements without an explicit scope decision.

## 3. University Ambassador Program — working concept

### 3.1 Concept

Invite motivated students to represent AZ within their university or student network. Each ambassador receives a unique referral link and, optionally, a referral code. When a new student joins through that link/code and completes defined milestones, the ambassador earns points.

The program could create a student-led discovery loop:
- An ambassador introduces AZ to classmates or relevant student communities.
- A new student registers through the ambassador's referral.
- The new student completes an activation milestone and experiences real product value.
- If the student later becomes a paying subscriber, the ambassador may earn additional points.
- Points unlock recognition, status, or carefully selected rewards.

### 3.2 Proposed referral milestones (not final)

| Event | Possible treatment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Referred visitor clicks | Analytics only; no points | Too easy to manipulate |
| New account verified | Small or no points | Confirms a real account, but not product value |
| New student reaches activation | Main referral points | Rewards a meaningful first experience |
| Referred student becomes a paid subscriber | Bonus points after payment is confirmed | Aligns rewards with sustainable revenue |
| Subscriber remains paid beyond a defined period | Optional retention bonus | Discourages low-quality short-lived referrals |

Define "activation" using real product behavior, not just login. A candidate definition to test is: verified account + completes onboarding + adds a subject or task + successfully uses one core study workflow. The final definition must fit the shipped product and avoid collecting unnecessary data.

### 3.3 Ambassador levels and rewards

Possible tier structure (names and thresholds to research):
- **Member / Explorer:** joins the program and completes onboarding.
- **Campus Ambassador:** consistently refers activated students and participates in approved activities.
- **Senior Ambassador:** demonstrates sustained, high-quality referrals and responsible representation.
- **AZ Campus Lead:** helps coordinate a campus group or approved campus activation.

Potential reward categories:
- AZ subscription credits or plan extensions.
- Recognition, certificates, profile status, and public appreciation (opt-in).
- Exclusive learning/product experiences or early access.
- Partner offers and student discounts, where legitimate and valuable.
- A future **AZ Shop** or partner-shop voucher/code, only if the economics, fulfillment, and demand make sense.

Do not promise cash, merchandise, shop codes, or fixed reward values until costs, local rules, tax/accounting implications, and operational capacity have been reviewed.

### 3.4 Fraud prevention and fairness

Research and design safeguards for:
- Self-referrals and duplicate accounts.
- Fake, bulk-created, or unverified accounts.
- Payment reversals, refunds, and chargebacks.
- Referral attribution conflicts and cookie/link expiry.
- Spam, unsolicited messages, and misleading promotion.
- Disputes, point expiry, reward caps, and program termination.
- Clear disclosure when ambassadors receive benefits for referrals.

Points should be pending until the qualifying event is verified. Publish understandable eligibility rules and a fair appeal process. Do not expose another student's private activity or academic data to the ambassador.

### 3.5 Pilot approach

Do not build a full ambassador platform before validating demand. A low-cost pilot could use:
1. A small, selected group of students across a few Qatar universities or faculties.
2. Unique referral links/codes and a simple, privacy-conscious tracking process.
3. A short pilot period with clear rules and modest rewards.
4. Weekly tracking of referred visitors, verified registrations, activated students, paid conversions, retention, fraud, and reward cost.
5. Interviews with ambassadors and referred students.

Proceed only if the program creates incremental, retained users at an acceptable cost and does not damage trust.

## 4. Other growth channels to research

These are research tracks, not commitments. Evaluate each for student fit, cost, effort, credibility, and measurable outcomes.

### 4.1 Student-led content and creator partnerships
- Student creators showing authentic study workflows and real product use.
- Short demonstrations using sample or permission-cleared academic material.
- Transparent sponsored content and referral disclosures.
- Campus-specific creators rather than reach-only influencer deals.

### 4.2 Campus societies and university partnerships
- Student clubs, academic societies, peer-learning groups, and approved events.
- Workshops on planning, active recall, and learning from mistakes, with AZ as a useful tool rather than the entire event.
- Small campus pilots before formal institutional partnerships.
- Respect university policies and obtain permission before using names, logos, venues, or mailing lists.

### 4.3 Product-led sharing
- Shareable study plans, progress milestones, or practice sets only where privacy, copyright, and academic integrity permit.
- Invite flows that are useful to both the sender and recipient.
- Collaborative or group features only after product scope and moderation requirements are reviewed; these are not part of the current MVP.

### 4.4 Referral loops and launch offers
- Student-to-student referral links/codes.
- Limited, clearly explained launch benefits.
- Group or cohort offers only when unit economics are understood.
- Avoid discounting as the only reason to use AZ.

### 4.5 Search, educational content, and community presence
- Useful Arabic and English content around study planning, PDF-based learning, practice, and mistake review.
- Search-focused guides answering real student questions.
- Helpful participation in existing student communities, following their rules and without unsolicited promotion.
- Landing pages tailored to legitimate student needs, not thin pages created only to capture search traffic.

### 4.6 PR, launches, and distinctive campaigns
- A clear launch story: the academic problem AZ solves and why its approach is different.
- Demonstrations, student stories with consent, and evidence from the beta.
- Thoughtful campus launch moments or public challenges that are safe, accessible, and relevant.
- Avoid stunts that create attention without product value or put students under pressure.

### 4.7 Partnerships and distribution
- Student discounts or bundles with relevant education services.
- Partnerships with tutoring, stationery, student support, or campus organizations where incentives are transparent.
- Explore institutional distribution only after reliability, privacy, support, and procurement expectations are understood.

## 5. Funnel and measurement

Use a consistent funnel so campaigns can be compared:

1. **Reach:** qualified students who actually see the message.
2. **Visit:** landing-page visitors or referral-link visits.
3. **Signup:** verified new accounts.
4. **Activation:** students who complete the agreed first-value milestone.
5. **Retention:** activated students who return and complete meaningful academic actions.
6. **Paid conversion:** students who become paying subscribers.
7. **Referral:** retained students who successfully invite others.

Candidate metrics:
- Visitor-to-signup conversion.
- Signup-to-activation conversion.
- Activation-to-retention by cohort.
- Paid conversion and paid retention.
- Referred-user activation and paid conversion.
- Incremental cost per activated student and per retained/paying student.
- Ambassador reward cost per incremental retained student.
- Fraud, refund, complaint, and unsubscribe rates.

Do not optimize for downloads, impressions, raw registrations, time spent, or notification opens in isolation. Define a North Star metric only after the first 15–50 beta students provide evidence.

## 6. Research plan — work with Claude and independent sources

Before committing to a channel or reward model, conduct a global research sprint. Use Claude as a research collaborator, then verify important claims against original sources.

Research questions:
- Which student ambassador programs have worked for education products, student tools, and SaaS products?
- What are the mechanics, eligibility rules, rewards, costs, and fraud controls of credible referral programs?
- Which programs publish evidence of retention or paid conversion, rather than only sign-up counts?
- What are the strongest campus distribution models in different regions?
- How do student preferences and university rules differ across Qatar, the GCC, the UK, the US, Europe, and other relevant markets?
- Which creator, SEO, community, partnership, and PR approaches have credible evidence?
- What legal, privacy, advertising-disclosure, consumer-protection, payment, and tax considerations apply in Qatar and target expansion markets?
- Which tactics are feasible for a small founder-led team with limited time and budget?

Research method:
1. Build a shortlist of relevant products and programs across multiple regions.
2. Prefer primary sources: official program terms, company case studies, university policies, public filings, and direct interviews.
3. Use independent reporting and credible research to challenge company-reported success claims.
4. Record source links, publication dates, geography, sample size, and evidence quality.
5. Separate verified facts, company claims, expert opinions, and our hypotheses.
6. Compare tactics by expected student value, effort, cost, risk, scalability, and measurement quality.
7. Turn findings into a ranked experiment backlog; do not treat popularity as proof of effectiveness.

### Reusable research brief for Claude

"Act as a skeptical growth researcher for AZ, an early-stage academic productivity and AI study platform launching first in Qatar. Research globally proven student acquisition and referral strategies, especially university ambassador programs. Find primary sources and credible independent evidence. For every example, document the product/company, geography, program mechanics, eligibility, reward structure, attribution method, fraud controls, reported outcomes, evidence quality, source links, and publication dates. Distinguish verified evidence from company claims and speculation. Identify lessons that could transfer to a bootstrapped, founder-led product with a small budget. Do not recommend copying a program without checking local legal, privacy, university-policy, and unit-economic constraints. Finish with a comparison matrix and a prioritized list of low-cost experiments for AZ."

## 7. Phased execution

### Before and during the beta
- Interview students and learn how they currently discover and recommend study tools.
- Test positioning and landing-page messages.
- Recruit a small number of beta advocates manually if useful.
- Track activation and retention before scaling acquisition.
- Keep incentives simple; do not build a points economy prematurely.

### After evidence of product value
- Pilot the ambassador program with transparent rules.
- Test referral links/codes and verified milestone rewards.
- Compare ambassador acquisition with content, campus partnerships, and creator channels.
- Add automation only when manual operations become a real bottleneck.

### After repeatable economics
- Expand to more campuses and markets.
- Consider an AZ Shop or partner reward catalogue.
- Develop ambassador tiers, dashboards, and program operations.
- Scale paid campaigns only when retention and payback support them.

## 8. Decisions still open

- Final ambassador name, tiers, and eligibility.
- Definition of an activated referred student.
- Points per milestone and any caps/expiry.
- Whether subscription credits, partner vouchers, cash, or shop codes are appropriate.
- Referral attribution window and conflict resolution.
- Pilot universities, cohort size, duration, and budget.
- Legal, privacy, advertising disclosure, tax, and payment review.
- North Star metric and acquisition/retention thresholds.
- Which channels merit a pilot after global research.

## 9. Guardrails

- No tactic enters the AZ V1 scope merely because it appears in this document.
- No rewards or paid campaigns are launched without founder approval and an affordability check.
- No claims of guaranteed grades, exam prediction, or unsupported learning outcomes.
- No spam, dark patterns, fake urgency, or undisclosed paid endorsements.
- Respect student privacy, academic integrity, copyright, university policies, and local law.
- Prefer a small measured experiment over a large unvalidated campaign.

---

**Working principle:** Make AZ worth recommending first. Then make recommending it rewarding, transparent, and measurable.
