# AZ — Project Purpose & Goals
> Status: Foundational direction — draft for founder review  
> Last updated: 2026-11-04

## 1. Why AZ Exists

AZ exists to help students take control of their academic journey.

Students often manage their academic life across disconnected tools: notes, PDFs, calendars, task lists, question banks, timers, and progress trackers. This fragmentation creates friction between knowing what needs to be done and actually learning, practicing, and improving.

AZ aims to bring that journey into one coherent, intelligent workspace.

The ambition is not to make students dependent on a product through lock-in or artificial barriers. It is to earn a meaningful place in their daily academic lives by being genuinely useful, trustworthy, and consistently valuable.

## 2. Vision

To become the **Academic Operating System** that helps every learner organize, understand, practice, and improve throughout their educational journey.

This is a long-term direction, not a promise that every educational stage or feature will be supported in the first release.

## 3. Mission

Help students turn their own academic materials, goals, and available time into a clear cycle of learning and improvement:

**Plan → Study → Practice → Review mistakes → Improve**

AZ should reduce the friction between intention and action, while keeping the student in control of their learning.

## 4. The Promise to Students

AZ should strive to be:

- **A clear starting point:** help students understand what matters next.
- **A reliable study companion:** make their own learning materials easier to work with.
- **A practice partner:** help them test understanding and learn from mistakes.
- **A progress mirror:** make effort, consistency, and areas for improvement more visible.
- **A trusted workspace:** treat their data with care and explain AI limitations honestly.

AZ supports learning; it does not replace the student's judgment, effort, teachers, or institution.

## 5. Strategic Goals

### 5.1 Become a trusted part of the student's academic routine

Students should return because AZ helps them make meaningful progress, not because their data is trapped or leaving is deliberately difficult.

### 5.2 Connect the full academic workflow

Bring planning, studying, practice, mistake review, and progress into a coherent experience rather than a collection of disconnected features.

### 5.3 Make each student's materials more useful

Help students transform their own lecture slides, PDFs, notes, and permitted past papers into understandable explanations and useful practice.

Generated answers and questions should be grounded in available source material where possible, with page references when supported. AZ must never claim to predict a professor's actual exam.

### 5.4 Make progress visible and actionable

Show students what they have completed, what needs attention, where they are struggling, and what a sensible next step could be.

### 5.5 Earn trust through quality and transparency

Prioritize privacy, secure data handling, clear AI limitations, reliable functionality, accessibility, and honest communication.

### 5.6 Build a product that can grow responsibly

Start with a focused, useful web MVP for the initial Qatar market. Expand to more learners, educational stages, platforms, and capabilities only when evidence and resources justify it.

## 6. Product Principles

1. **Student benefit over feature count.** Every feature must solve a real student problem.
2. **Learning over dependency.** AZ should strengthen the student's ability to learn independently.
3. **Clarity over complexity.** The next useful action should be easy to understand.
4. **Trust over short-term engagement.** Never use manipulative patterns, artificial lock-in, or misleading claims to increase usage.
5. **Evidence over assumptions.** Validate important decisions with real students and observed behavior.
6. **Useful AI, honestly presented.** AI output can be wrong; communicate uncertainty and preserve source context where possible.
7. **Performance is part of quality.** The product must remain responsive on modest devices and connections.
8. **Accessibility and inclusion.** Support Arabic and English, RTL and LTR, keyboard and touch interaction, and reduced motion.
9. **Focused execution.** Protect the MVP from unnecessary scope expansion.
10. **Student ownership.** Respect student privacy and provide clear ways to access and delete their data.

## 7. What Success Should Mean

Success is not simply downloads, time spent in the app, or the number of AI generations.

We should measure whether AZ helps students build a more effective and sustainable academic routine.

Potential indicators to validate:

- Students who return and complete meaningful academic actions over time.
- Planning tasks completed and focus sessions meaningfully used.
- Study materials successfully processed and used for learning.
- Practice attempts followed by mistake review or another learning action.
- Students who report that AZ helps them feel more organized and better prepared.
- Retention, referrals, and willingness to pay, interpreted alongside actual student value.
- Reliability, performance, accessibility, privacy, and AI cost per useful outcome.

The exact North Star Metric and metric definitions remain **to be validated** with the first 15–50 test students. Do not optimize for screen time or notification opens as goals by themselves.

## 8. What AZ Is Not Trying to Be

- Not a tool that promises exam predictions or guaranteed grades.
- Not a replacement for teachers, universities, or the student's own thinking.
- Not a social network or community platform in the MVP.
- Not a collection of AI features without a coherent learning workflow.
- Not a product that creates dependence through lock-in, guilt, or manipulative engagement.
- Not a platform that expands to every learner segment before serving its initial students well.

## 9. Long-Term Ambition vs. Current MVP

The Academic Operating System vision should guide direction, but it must not be mistaken for the V1 delivery scope.

V1 remains focused on the agreed MVP: core planning and focus, PDF upload and extraction, summaries and multiple-choice practice grounded in student materials, mistake review, usage limits and AI cost logging, and account deletion.

Any expansion beyond the approved scope requires explicit review and approval. The project specification remains the source of truth for implementation requirements.

## 10. Decision Filter

Before approving a major feature or product decision, ask:

1. Does this solve a real academic problem?
2. Does it strengthen the Plan → Study → Practice → Review → Improve cycle?
3. Does it help students become more capable, rather than more dependent?
4. Can we deliver it reliably, securely, accessibly, and affordably?
5. Is there evidence that students need it now?
6. Does it fit the approved stage and scope of AZ?

If the answer is unclear, research and validate before committing.

---

**Founding idea:** AZ should become indispensable through the value it earns, not through dependence it manufactures.
